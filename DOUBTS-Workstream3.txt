1) How the Overall SQS Score Is Calculated

The SQS headline score (0-100) is a weighted average of six individual pillar scores. Each pillar carries a specific weight:

Structural Completeness - 25%
Exposure Consistency - 25%
Property Integrity - 15%
Loss History - 15%
Umbrella Adequacy -10%
Narrative Quality -10%

Each pillar is calculated independently using its own logic. Here is exactly how each one works.

1. Structural Completeness (25%)

This pillar measures whether the core application information is present and the forms are filled in properly. It is made up of three weighted components:

Core Application Fields (35% of this pillar) - Is the applicant name, mailing address, effective date, contact information, producer name, and lines of business present?
Underwriting Profile Fields (30% of this pillar) - Are the deeper business facts present? This includes FEIN, entity type, operations description, annual revenue, payroll, number of employees, years in business, prior carrier, NAICS code, number of prior claims, and workers comp modifier.
Form Fill Quality (35% of this pillar) - Across the ACORD forms that were generated, how completely were the fields filled in and how confident are we in those values? Producer-entered values score at 100%, AI-high-confidence values at 85%, and AI-low-confidence values at 50%.
I generally agree with the current logic for V1.
My only concern is that we are combining three different concepts into one score:
• Information Presence
• Information Quality
• AI Confidence
This can create confusion because a user may see that all required information is present but still receive a lower score due to AI confidence levels.
For V1:
Keep the current calculation if it is already built.
For a future release:
Separate Extraction Confidence from SQS.
Example:
SQS = 88
Extraction Confidence = 92%
This will make the score easier for users to understand and trust.
Recommended Structure
Applicant Information (30%)
• Named Insured
• Address
• Contact Information
• Effective Date
• Entity Type
Underwriting Information (40%)
• Revenue
• Payroll
• Employees
• Years in Business
• Operations Description
• FEIN
• NAICS
Supporting Documents (30%)
• Loss Runs
• Dec Pages
• Schedule of Values (SOV)
• Prior Policies
• Supplemental Applications
Long-Term Recommendation
Structural Completeness should answer one question: "Did we collect the information needed to evaluate the risk?"
Extraction Confidence should answer a separate question: "How confident are we that the extracted information is correct?"
Keeping those as separate metrics will prevent confusion and make the scoring easier to explain.

2. Exposure Consistency (25%)

This pillar measures whether the business exposure data is complete and internally consistent. It starts at 100 and applies deductions for each gap found:

No GL class codes detected: - 20 points
Class codes present but no operations description: - 10 points
No GL limits found: -8 points
No payroll or revenue in the submission: -15 points
Workers Comp detected but no WC payroll: -12 points
Workers Comp detected but no class codes: -10 points
Multi-state WC with no state-by-state breakdown: -8 points
Contractor submission with no subcontracting percentage: -8 points
Auto coverage but no auto liability limit: -10 points
Auto coverage but no covered symbols defined: -5 points
Cross-form conflicts found: up to -20 points
This is one of the most important pillars and should remain heavily weighted. I agree with the current deductions for:

3. Property Integrity (15%)

This pillar is entirely driven by COPE (Construction, Occupancy, Protection, Exposure) data from the uploaded documents. No form fill quality component is involved.

If no property coverage is detected - 100 (not applicable)
Minimum Viable COPE - All four of the following must be present: location address, occupancy type, construction type, and either building value or BPP value. If any of these are missing → 0 (hard stop)
If Minimum Viable COPE passes, we then check 10 Carrier-Grade fields: year built, roof year, sprinkler system, fire protection class, valuation method, distance to hydrant, fire department type, protection class, business income limit, and period of restoration
All 10 present - 100
Some missing - score between 60 and 70 (never higher until all carrier-grade fields are complete)

4. Loss History (15%)

This pillar is driven by the loss run documents uploaded and the loss data extracted from them. It uses a tiered scoring model:

5 or more years of current loss runs + prior carrier name - 100
5 or more years, no prior carrier - 80
3 to 4 years of loss runs - 75
1 to 2 years - 40
Loss run document uploaded but years not yet confirmed:
Name + FEIN or policy number match the insured - 50 (strong match)
Name matches only - 35 (possible match, ownership warning issued)
Name does not match - 15 (no match)
User or narrative attests no prior losses, no loss runs → 60
No loss information at all - 10

Additional adjustments applied after the base score:
Loss runs older than 90 days - up to -25 points, with a warning
High claim frequency relative to revenue/payroll exposure -> -10 to -25 points
Loss ratio above 10% of exposure -> -15 points

This is an important pillar, but loss runs should not be a hard requirement for a submission to be considered Quote Ready.
In the real world:
• New ventures may have no loss history
• Loss runs are often pending from incumbent carriers
• Brokers frequently submit while waiting for updated loss runs
• Insureds may attest to no known losses
Because of this, missing loss runs should impact the score, but should not automatically prevent a submission from being marketed or quoted.
Recommended Scoring
• 5 years current loss runs + prior carrier identified = 100
• 3-4 years current loss runs = 80
• 1-2 years current loss runs = 40
• Loss runs requested / pending = 70
• Insured attests no prior losses = 60
• No loss information provided = 25
Ownership Validation
Do not rely on business name alone.
Use the following match hierarchy:
Strong Match
• Name + FEIN
• Name + Policy Number
Moderate Match
• Name + Address
Weak Match
• Name Only
Apply confidence scoring based on the strength of the match.
Additional Validation
Prior Carrier Present
• +10 points
Prior Carrier Missing
• -10 points
Prior carrier information is important underwriting information and is commonly found on loss runs and prior policy documents.
Loss Runs Older Than 90 Days
• Warning and score reduction
High Claim Frequency
• Warning and score reduction
Focus on claim frequency, not just claim dollars. Multiple small claims can be a greater underwriting concern than a single large claim.
The goal of this pillar is to reward complete and current loss information while recognizing that some accounts can still be marketed and quoted before final loss runs are received.

6. Narrative Quality (10%)

This pillar scores the quality and completeness of the underwriting narrative - specifically the ACORD 101 remarks field, or the operations description as a fallback.

Scoring is a blend of two elements:

Component Coverage (60% of this pillar) - How many of the 12 narrative components are present? The system scans the narrative text for: Account Overview, Operations Description, Years in Business, Management Experience, Risk Controls, Loss History Discussion, Coverage Discussion, Prior Carrier Context, Location Details, Employee/Payroll Context, WC Payroll/Class Context, and EMOD/XMOD information.
Text Quality (40% of this pillar) - Is the narrative substantive and specific? This is measured by text length (up to 300 characters scored) and vocabulary diversity (unique words vs. total words).
Additional notes:

If the narrative comes from the operations description field rather than a dedicated ACORD 101 narrative, the score is reduced by 25% - we treat a full narrative as higher quality than an operations description alone.
If a narrative document is uploaded and classified, the score is floored at 40 regardless of text content - we credit the fact that a narrative was provided even if the specific text did not extract fully.
If no narrative is present at all -> 0

1.1) THE CURRENT DISCONNECT

When a producer clicks to expand any of the six pillars, they see a set of sub-rows. For example, under Structural Completeness they might see:

Applicant Information - 100%
Entity Information - 100%
Effective Date Consistency - 100%
Policy Term Consistency - 100%
Supporting Documentation - 66%
The problem is that these sub-rows are a separate display layer. They show whether individual pieces of information are present or missing, but they are not the inputs that produced the headline score. They were built as an explanation tool to help producers understand what's in the submission, not as a rollup of the number above them.

The result is a disconnect that can be confusing: all five sub-rows can show 100% while the pillar score still shows 65, because the pillar score is also being driven by underwriting profile fields and form fill quality - neither of which appears in the sub-rows.

Option to Resolve This: 

Keep the current calculation, add a tooltip to explain it.

The scoring math stays exactly as it is today. We add a small label or tooltip under each pillar headline explaining that the score is driven by the full weighted formula, and that the expandable sub-rows are a field-by-field presence guide rather than a direct rollup.
I do NOT think a tooltip alone solves the problem. If users see:
Applicant Information = 100%
Entity Information = 100%
Effective Date Consistency = 100%
Policy Term Consistency = 100%
But the pillar score shows 65, they will assume the platform is wrong.
My recommendation:
Either:
Option A (Preferred)
Make the subcategories reflect the actual scoring inputs.
or
Option B
Rename them:
"Field Presence Indicators"
Instead of displaying them as if they are scoring components.
