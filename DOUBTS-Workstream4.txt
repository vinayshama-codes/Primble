DOUBTS-Workstream 4 - ACORD Form Recommendation Logic-  AND SOLUTIONS BY CLIENT 

 
MY EXPLAINATION TO CLIENT  : This is how we have implemented the suggested tier assignments. Please let us know if we are moving in the right direction.
  
REQUIRED
A form is Required when both of the following are true:
The LLM confirmed the coverage exists in the uploaded documents.
The form is the primary form for that coverage line.
ACORD 125 is always Required because it is the master application and no submission can exist without it. For all other forms, Required means the documents showed the coverage is active and the form is the primary form used to capture that coverage.
Examples:
If General Liability is confirmed - ACORD 126 is Required.
If Business Auto is confirmed - ACORD 127 is Required.
If Workers Compensation is confirmed - ACORD 130 is Required.
If Umbrella / Excess Liability is confirmed - ACORD 131 is Required.
If Commercial Property is confirmed - ACORD 140 is Required.
 
RECOMMENDED
A form is Recommended when the submission contains confirmed coverage or operations, and the form provides supplemental or supporting information. These forms do not establish a new coverage line; they add detail to an already confirmed one.
Examples:
Contractor operations are confirmed - ACORD 186  is Recommended.
Commercial Property coverage is confirmed and multiple locations, schedules, or valuation details exist - ACORD 141 is Recommended.
An active construction project is detected - ACORD 133  is Recommended.
 
OPTIONAL
A form is Optional when there is a legitimate reason it may be useful, but its inclusion depends on broker judgment and the specific submission.
Examples:
Data conflicts, unclear operations, unusual financial ratios, missing context, or cross-validation issues require additional explanation - ACORD 101  is Optional.
Equipment floater, inland marine, transportation, or cargo-related keywords are found but are not confirmed as a primary coverage line - ACORD 160 is Optional.
 
NEEDS CONFIRMATION
A form is assigned Needs Confirmation in any of the following situations:

Situation 1 - Soft Signal, Not Confirmed
Evidence of the coverage exists, but the LLM did not positively confirm it.
Example:
A declarations page shows "General Liability – $1,000,000", but structured extraction did not classify the coverage as confirmed.
ACORD 126 is assigned Needs Confirmation pending broker review.
Situation 2 - Logical Implication, Not Direct Evidence
A confirmed form or operation strongly suggests another form should exist, but that coverage was not directly identified in the uploaded documents.
Example:
ACORD 186 is confirmed.
General Liability was not directly detected.
Because contractor operations typically require General Liability coverage, ACORD 126 is assigned Needs Confirmation.
Situation 3 - Uploaded Source Document
The broker uploaded an existing ACORD form as part of the submission.
This serves as evidence of existing coverage rather than a form that necessarily needs to be generated.
Example:
The broker uploads an existing ACORD 25 Certificate of Insurance.
The system identifies it as a source document.
ACORD 25 is assigned Needs Confirmation with the note:
 "Source document detected - generate a clean copy only if needed."


Question 1 -  The Required vs Recommended boundary

Our logic is: if the coverage is confirmed on the documents, its primary form is Required. If a form supplements a confirmed coverage rather than capturing a new one, it is Recommended.
Does this match how underwriters prioritise forms in a submission packet? Specifically,  if a submission has confirmed GL, Auto, WC, and Umbrella, would an underwriter treat all four line forms (126, 127, 130, 131) as equally mandatory? Or would some of these be considered secondary depending on the risk type?

CLIENT ANSWER :
Required vs Recommended
I agree with your logic.If coverage is confirmed and the form is the primary form for that coverage, it should be Required.
Examples:
• GL confirmed = ACORD 126 Required
• Auto confirmed = ACORD 127 Required
• WC confirmed = ACORD 130 Required
• Umbrella confirmed = ACORD 131 Required
• Property confirmed = ACORD 140 Required
From an underwriting standpoint, these primary line forms are all equally important if the coverage exists.
Recommended forms should remain supplemental forms:
• ACORD 186
• ACORD 141
• ACORD 133
One suggestion:
ACORD 101 should not always be Optional.
If Primble detects:
• Large losses
• Data conflicts
• Missing context
• Prior carrier issues
• Unusual operations

Then ACORD 101 should automatically move to Recommended. A good narrative often helps an underwriter understand the account.

Should confirmed auto coverage in a confirmed CA or CO insured automatically promote ACORD 137 CA / ACORD 137 CO to Required? Or is Needs Confirmation the right default for state-specific supplemental forms regardless of how much evidence we have?

CLIENT ANSWER : I would keep ACORD 137 and 138 in Needs Confirmation. Do not automatically move them to Required. Even if Auto is confirmed and the insured is located in Colorado or California, not every carrier requires these forms.

2) ACORD Form Prior Carrier Issues

MY QUESTION : Primble currently extracts the prior carrier's name from uploaded documents. However, it does not yet extract the reason the insured left that carrier, for example, whether the policy was nonrenewed, cancelled, or the carrier declined to quote.

This distinction matters before we build the trigger. An account that switched carriers voluntarily is a routine submission. An account that was nonrenewed or cancelled for losses is a risk that underwriters will specifically look for narrative on. Treating both the same way would either over-trigger ACORD 101 on clean accounts or under-trigger it on the ones that genuinely need a narrative.

Before we proceed, we need your guidance on two points:

1. What counts as a "prior carrier issue" for this trigger?
Nonrenewal or cancellation by the carrier only?
Also includes the carrier declining to quote at renewal?
Or should ACORD 101 escalate any time a prior carrier is mentioned, regardless of the reason?

CLIENT ANSWER :
I would NOT trigger ACORD 101 simply because a prior carrier is present.  Almost every submission has a prior carrier, so that would create a lot of unnecessary ACORD 101 forms.  I would only trigger ACORD 101 when there is evidence of a meaningful underwriting concern.
Examples:
Trigger ACORD 101:
• Nonrenewal by carrier
• Cancellation by carrier
• Carrier declined renewal
• Carrier exited the market
• Coverage restrictions imposed by carrier
• Significant adverse loss history
• Broker indicates there is a carrier issue
Do NOT automatically trigger ACORD 101 for:
• Shopping the market
• Premium increase
• Broker change
• Voluntary carrier change
• Seeking broader coverage
• Seeking better pricing
Those are normal marketplace activities and usually do not require an underwriting explanation.  For V1, I would recommend a simple producer/client question when we cannot determine the reason.
Example:
Why are you seeking coverage?
Select one:
• Shopping for better pricing
• Coverage concerns
• Carrier nonrenewal
• Carrier cancellation
• Carrier declined renewal
• Carrier exited market
• New venture
• Other
If Other:
Please explain.
This information can automatically improve the Narrative Quality score and determine whether ACORD 101 should be recommended.

MY QUESTION 2. How reliably does this information appear in the documents brokers actually upload?
Prior carrier names typically appear on dec pages and ACORD 125 applications. The reason for leaving - nonrenewal, cancellation, market exit - usually only appears in loss run cover letters, submission narratives, or the broker's own notes. If this detail rarely makes it into the uploaded documents, the trigger may not fire often enough to justify building it now.

CLIENT ANSWER : Prior carrier names appear frequently. Common sources:
• Loss runs
• Dec pages
• ACORD 125
• Prior policy documents
However, the reason for leaving the carrier is much less reliable. Sometimes it appears in:
• Broker narratives
• Submission emails
• ACORD 101 remarks
• Loss run cover letters
• Producer notes
But most of the time it does not appear in the documents at all. Because of that, I would not build V1 assuming Primble can reliably determine why the insured is leaving the carrier. I would not spend development time trying to detect carrier issues from documents alone.
Instead:
Extract when available:
• Prior carrier
• Policy expiration date
• Loss history
Then ask:
Why are you marketing this account?
Any prior carrier issues?
Any upcoming deadlines or urgency?
Those answers can:
• Trigger ACORD 101
• Improve Narrative Quality
• Improve Submission Readiness
• Give underwriters valuable context
This will be much more reliable than trying to infer carrier issues from uploaded documents.
This is actually a great example of where a targeted questionnaire is better than AI. The insured or producer can answer those three questions in 15 seconds, and you'll get far more reliable information than trying to extract it from documents that often don't contain it.

Overall Recommendation
I would simplify the scoring structure and make it behave exactly how an underwriter thinks. Today we are mixing into the same buckets:
Presence of data
Quality of data
Coverage adequacy
Underwriting readiness
I would keep the six pillars but make them easier to understand and easier to explain.
Proposed SQS Framework (Overall Score = Weighted Average)
Structural Completeness - 25%
Exposure Consistency - 25%
Property Integrity - 15%
Loss History - 15%
Umbrella / Excess Adequacy - 10%
Narrative Quality - 10%



For V1 I would focus on:
SQS
Readiness Status
Gap Detection
Remediation Workflow
ACORD Recommendations
I would not overcomplicate the scoring model. The producer only wants to know, "What do I need to fix?" That is ultimately what will make SQS valuable and trusted. My overall assessment is that 80-90% of the logic is very good for V1. The biggest changes I'd make are:
Loss runs should impact readiness differently than they impact scoring.
Don't rely on a tooltip to explain score discrepancies.
Don't overbuild prior carrier issue detection.
Keep SQS simple enough that a producer immediately understands how to improve it.
